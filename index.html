Five icons that use the light above white

Each specimen below is ordinary dark UI with one small region encoded in Rec.2100 HLG, so it renders above SDR paper white wherever the display has headroom to spare.

Turn your screen brightness down to roughly half and view in a dark room. Headroom is the gap above your current white level — at full brightness there is almost none left, and the effect flattens.

Ember

A matte ceramic tile with a hairline fracture that reads as molten rather than painted. The stone stays dull; only the crack carries light.

Recipe. 2.4px stroke at roughly +2 stops, a 1px inner stroke a little hotter still, and an SDR blur beneath it. The blur does the atmosphere, the HDR stroke does the source.

Recording

Status by luminance instead of size or saturation. A 9px dot that outshines the label pulls the eye from across a room, and survives being placed on light or dark backgrounds.

Recipe. One dot at about +1 stop, slow opacity pulse, soft SDR corona. The pill and text stay entirely in standard range so nothing else competes.

Filament

The outline is drawn in dim grey; the coil inside is the only thing above white. It stops looking like a picture of a bulb and starts looking like a bulb that is on.

Recipe. Glass at 14% white, filament at the headroom ceiling, wide SDR halo at 30%. Total emissive area is under 2% of the icon.

Bevel

A glass tile whose top edge catches a light source that is not in the frame. This is the most transferable pattern — any existing icon can take one specular streak without a redesign.

Recipe. A 3px highlight running to the headroom ceiling at its centre and falling to transparent at both ends, rotated with the bevel. Blur behind, hard edge in front.

Sweep

A progress ring where brightness, not just colour, travels along the arc. The leading tip crosses into headroom so completion feels like something arriving rather than a bar filling.

Recipe. One gradient stroke, standard range for most of its length, crossing paper white only in the final third. Keep the transition slow or the tip looks detached.

What to watch for

  • Every specimen is legible with the clamp on. If an icon needs the boost to be readable, it is broken on most screens.
  • The emissive area is always small. Large bright regions read as a rendering fault, not as light.
  • The pairing that sells the illusion is a hard HDR core with a soft standard-range bloom around it. HDR alone looks flat; bloom alone looks like a sticker.
  • Low Power Mode, the Reduce White Point setting, a hot phone, and high screen brightness all shrink your headroom. Design for the boost to disappear.

Browser support, honestly

Safari on Apple hardware is the reliable path — it maps Rec.2100 CSS colours into extended dynamic range. Chrome on Windows HDR has improved but is inconsistent, and the effect never appears on a display with no headroom. The readout at the top tells you what your current browser admits to.
