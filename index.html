Five icons that use the light above white

The glow here comes from a real HDR asset — a tiny Rec.2100 image and video, both encoded above SDR reference white — masked into the shape of each icon. Everything else is ordinary CSS.

Read this first. If either right-hand patch looks brighter than the pure white one, that source is reaching your display's headroom — pick it below. If both match the white patch, this iPhone isn't granting headroom to the page and nothing further down will glow.

Ember

A matte ceramic tile with a hairline fracture that reads as molten rather than painted. The stone stays dull; only the crack carries light.

Recipe. An amber HDR source masked by an angular stroked path with a branch at 70% opacity, so the branch runs cooler, over a soft standard-range blur. The blur makes the atmosphere, the masked HDR makes the source.

Recording

Status by luminance instead of size or saturation. A 10px dot that outshines its own label pulls the eye from across a room and works on any background.

Recipe. One radial mask on a rose HDR source, with a standard-range corona behind it. The pill and text never leave standard range, so nothing competes.

Filament

The glass is drawn in dim grey; only the coil sits above white. It stops looking like a picture of a bulb and starts looking like a bulb that is on.

Recipe. Glass at 14% white, coil masked from the brightest source, wide standard-range halo at 30%. Emissive area is under 2% of the icon.

Bevel

A glass tile catching a light source that is not in the frame. The most transferable pattern here — any existing icon can take one specular streak without a redesign.

Recipe. A linear-gradient mask over the HDR source, rotated with the bevel, blurred copy behind it. Hard edge in front, soft edge behind.

Sweep

A progress ring where brightness travels along the arc, not just colour. The leading tip crosses into headroom, so completion feels like something arriving rather than a bar filling.

Recipe. A gradient mask running from fully transparent to opaque, so the same HDR source fades from invisible through dim to above-white along one stroke.

What to watch for

  • Every icon is legible with the clamp on. If one needs the boost to be readable, it is broken on most screens.
  • The emissive area is always small. Large bright regions read as a rendering fault, not as light.
  • A hard HDR core with a soft standard-range bloom around it is what sells the illusion. HDR alone looks flat; bloom alone looks like a sticker.
  • Low Power Mode, Reduce White Point, a warm phone and high screen brightness all shrink your headroom.

How this is built

Two 300-byte assets carry all the light: a Rec.2100 PQ still and an HLG video clip, both encoded brighter than reference white and embedded directly in this file. Each icon masks one of them into a shape. That is the whole technique, and it is the same one used for glowing avatars and app icons in production.
