Two that use all of it

The ladder isolates techniques. These combine them into objects with implied physics — which is the only reason any of it stops looking like a lighting effect and starts looking like light.

Kindling

A ceramic tile with a hairline fracture. Heat travels along the crack rather than filling it; the room falls away as the heat builds, and comes back when it passes.

Everything at once. Nested masks (the crack holds the shape, a sliding gradient decides where the light is), a surround that darkens and cools through the travel, and an SDR bloom that leads the hot spot. Clamp it: the crack is still a crack.

Settled

A payment confirming. A ring collapses inward, and the moment it reaches the centre the whole surround drops out for a fifth of a second while a single point fires above white. Then four seconds of nothing.

Paid

The budget, spent once. The word is fully legible before, during and after — the light carries no information. Peak area is under 1% of the frame and lasts about 200ms, which is the one thing displays let you push hardest. Everything you have, once per event, then out of the way.

Now screenshot this page

Take a screenshot and open it. The glow is gone. That gap is the entire problem: this cannot exist in a Figma file, a spec, a design review deck, a marketing shot, an App Store screenshot, or a bug report. Every artefact your team passes around is a lossy capture of the thing you designed.

Then run the page again in daylight, at 20% brightness, in Low Power Mode, and with Reduce White Point on. Watch how much of your budget survives each one. That fragility, not the glow, is what to design around.

The rules this lab is testing

  • Legible when clamped. If a specimen needs the boost to be readable, it is broken on most screens and in every screenshot.
  • Boost is decoration, never information. Clamp permanently and nothing should change function.
  • Small area, short time. Large bright regions read as a rendering fault; sustained ones read as a mistake.
  • Hard HDR core, soft SDR bloom. HDR alone looks flat, bloom alone looks like a sticker.
  • Spend the budget once per screen. Two things competing for headroom means neither gets it.
  • Dim-then-flash is a photosensitivity pattern. Keep transitions under three per second, keep the area small, and honour prefers-reduced-motion — this page does.

Why the counter-dim matters more than the headroom

Your range is headroom stops plus however far you are willing to pull the surround down. The second half costs nothing, works on every display, survives clamping, and survives the screenshot. Set Counter-dim to 0 and walk the ladder again — most of what makes these specimens work disappears, and the headroom alone does not replace it.

How it is built

Four Rec.2100 stills and four matching HLG clips, a few hundred bytes each, encoded brighter than reference white and embedded in this file. Each specimen masks one of them into a shape. Nothing else here is exotic: the surround, the bloom, the dimming and the timing are ordinary CSS, and that is where most of the effect comes from.
